**Ahnentafel** translates to 'ancestor table' in German. It is an ascending numbering system to order and identify ancestors; <u>however, it only shows a direct line.</u> 
# Features
- Begins with a Subject (descendant) and works backwards through the generations. 
- Only shows the direct line. No siblings and no other children. 
- Male ancestors are even numbers and female ancestors are odd.
## Calculating Ancestor's Number
- To find any descendant's father's number = double the younger person's number
- To find any descendant's mother's number = double their own number, then add 1.
	- The mother will always be one number higher than the father. 

> [!example] Example from [English Ancestors Blog](https://englishancestors.blog/2020/08/01/the-ahnentafel-system/#:~:text=You%20are%20number,and%20so%20on.)
> You are number **1**  
> Your mother is number **3**   [1×2 =2 +1 =3]  
> Her father (your grandfather) is 3×2 =**6** and her mother (your maternal grandmother) is 3×2 =6 +1 =**7**  
> Your maternal grandmother’s mother (your mother’s mother’s mother) is therefore 7×2 =14 +1 =**15**  
> Your mother’s mother’s mother’s father is therefore 15×2 =**30**.  
> His father is **60**.  His father is **120** and the mother is **121**, and so on.

NOTE: This arrangement is applicable to all trees; however, numbers are not universal among all family members (i.e., cousins will differ).
## Generation Numbers

| Generation | Relationship to Subject | Ahnentafel (A-Numbers) | Worked Example w/ A-Number + Prefix            |
| ---------- | ----------------------- | ---------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| 1          | (Subject)               | 1                      | 1: 01                                          |
| 2          | Parents                 | 2-3                    | Father = 2 *or* 2: 02                          |
| 3          | Grandparents            | 4-7                    | Maternal Grandmother = 7 *or* 3: 07            |
| 4          | Great Grandparents      | 8-15                   | Mother's Father's Father (MFF) = 12 *or* 4: 12 |
| 5          | GG Grandparents         | 16-31                  | FMFF = 20 *or* 5: 20                           |
| 6          | GGG Grandparents        | 32-63                  | MMMMM = 63 *or* 6: 63                          |
| 7          | 4xG Grandparents        | 64-127                 | FFFMMF = 70 *or* 7: 70                         |
| 8          | 5xG Grandparents        | 128-255                | FFFFFFF = 128 *or* 8: 128                      |
| 9          | 6xG Grandparents        | 256-511                | MFMFFFMF = 418 *or* 9: 418                     |
| 10         | 7xG Grandparents        | 512-1103               | FMMMFFMF = 738 *or* 10: 738                    |
| etc.       | ...                     | ...                    | ...                                            |
## Pros: 
- Easier to pinpoint relationship of ancestor to subject with A-Number or shorthand.
- Organization / auto-indexation of ascending numbered system. 
## Cons: 
- Only able to list direct ancestors. 
	- See [[Dollarhide]] for siblings & half-siblings. 
- While appropriate for tracing ancestral roots through reproduction and DNA, this system DOES NOT account for same-sex couples, transgender or non-conforming identities, or enslaved relatives.
	- What are other ways to index individual research profiles that acknowledge the breadth of our relationships and where we know from?
