I remember how Mom sat on my right but did not take up space and I (later) thought about how her absence made supper-time quite lonesome.

I remember my black cat –not real of course– due to my extreme allergies, yet I yearned for for the vibrato of love from a small creature; so I would pet my toy cat until their back became worn to the stuffing. Petrified, I saw my cat’s internal filling. I remember thinking I was stuffed, too.

I remember, I remember, my first home. My neighbor, Eric. Shared initials and child-sized, apple red bikes from driveway to mine and now I think of how that memory is glorified with facts untrue: I wonder how Eric is coping.

I remember Dad’s classroom and the smell of the old paint and the stains of glaze and the paper that scattered itself on the dust-covered floors in the storage room because they became too heavy with their own weight. I remember when I first felt like the paper, metaphorically.

I remember when I shook with tears over the death of my childhood dog, the one who had grown with me into an old man while I remained twelve years old and how cold the bathroom floor feels against my bare legs. I think I was too young to know why I hid my sorrow, maybe because Dad wept in front of me and Mom hid for a week.

I remember the hug that shattered the soul of my ten-year-old self trapped by the shelter of my aged skin and I wish I could feel the intoxication of intolerable pain and confusion without consequence just once more.

I remember the time Mom looked at me with those eyes I have come to despise and her stare now reflects at me in my features with amort.

I remember my sister’s knock and her patterns of asking for entrance when she believes she occupies too much space and I’ll hug and kiss her head like I wish(ed) someone did for me, “_it is O.K. and you are welcomed. Always_” and she’ll wipe our tears before kissing my wet cheek and saying she loves me.

I remember when I asked for the time of day and was told _tomorrow_ by the Gods I do not believe in.

I remember when I thought I was everything.
I remember when I was nothing more.

I remember when I was cruel to myself and regretted each world and (later) felt sorry for my own conscience.

I remember that I cannot remember to take a break.

I remember that I have no idea where, who, what, everywhere and everything, all at once: I feel it.

**February 3, 2023**h
A part of the [[Portfolio/The Voice & Vision Chapbook/index|The Voice & Vision Chapbook]]; written in a classroom workshop with Alessandra Lynch, [inspired by Joe Brainard](https://www.poetryfoundation.org/articles/1597226/i-remember) in his book _I Remember_ (Granary Books, 2001).
