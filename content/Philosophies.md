# The English Language

## Proper Nouns

During my second year of undergraduate, I found that no longer aligned to my previous commitment and goals of Anthropology and Psychology; the latter was far too confined in it's registers of recognition. While I still believe in the scientific pursuit and much of my own research follows empirical reasoning, I believe that as Humans, the emotional realm confers an equal distribution of "understanding." Leaving my combined major, I embarked on a shortened, concentrated English degree in Literary Theory, Culture, and Criticism: I challenged every aspect of interdisciplinary thought by approaching my prior and subsequent teachings in Anthropology as a critical space to engage with the English Language.

My Grandfather, Tom Dorr, was an English teacher in the Eau Claire region for many decades and significantly influenced my decisions; yet, I felt inadequate in my own understanding of the linguistic conventions. My writing has been regarding as _bold_ and _defiant,_ yet I have always questioned how much of that arises from a deep misunderstanding rather than the belief of "learn the rules to break them." My subsequent years at [[Butler University]] were spent seeking both compliments and complacence; where does the self arise within the efforts to publish?

With many marks on my undergraduate papers, I continue to stand by my regard of language and the capitalization bestowed to "Proper Nouns." Challenging the beliefs of the individual versus the collective, I strongly believe that an attempt to communicate the varying bodies must be addressed through defying the English language delimited.

- Human
- World
- Nation
- Woman/Women
- Black
