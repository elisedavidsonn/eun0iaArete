> **March 27, 1915 Letter from Gertrude Mahorney:**  
> - “If you remember, my brother John bought a lot in Irvington; lot no. 8 in Chambers Addition Sub-division to Irvington. It faces on East Michigan St. I want to ask you what lots in that part of Irvington are now worth, as I am anxious to sell mine as soon as I can. Please tell me the highest price it will bring now or about the last of April or May. 
>   With best wishes for the College, I remain very sincerely yours, 
>   Gertrude A. Mahorney, 263 Eliot St., Detroit, Michigan” 
> 
> **April 23, 1915 Letter from President Howe:**  
> - “Dear Miss Mahorney:  
> 	 Your letter of March 27th, should have been answered before this, but I had waited until I might get a report in regard to the lot which you mentioned. Hence the delay. 
> 	I saw Mr. J. H. Badger, a most reliable real estate man, this morning and after he had looked up the lot information for me, he says that lots near there sold not long ago for $800.00. He says, also, that there is a very nice new bungalow house built recently on one side of your lot, while on the other side there is an older house which is something of a drawback to your property. He esteemed the value of your lot as about $750.00. He said for me to say to you that he should estimate it somewhere between $700.00 and $800.00. All of the ground on the north part of town where this is located is good and may enhance by degrees. My own judgement is that if I could get the price named by Mr. Badger, I should accept it. If you would like for Mr. Badger to take up the matter of selling it for you, I suggest that you write to him in care of the J. S. Spann & Company, Hume-Mansur Building. This is in my judgment the most reliable firm in Indianapolis. Mr. Badger is a many of most high character and would be trusted, in my judgement, to deal fairly. I should unhesitatingly place a property of my own in his hands. I, myself, am not buying any real estate these days so that I could not consider purchase at this time, otherwise, I should be glad to take it. 
> 	If I can serve you further in this matter, do not hesitate to call upon me. 
> 	Very truly yours, TCH/BH” 
> 
> **April 27, 1915 Letter from Gerturde Mahorney:**  
> - “Dear Sir – Your answer to my letter of inquiry received and I thank you very much for your kindness. I sent a letter to the Sec. of Butler College, asking if the College could aid me by buying my lot. I suppose you have seen the letter by this time. It would make me very happy if I could get more than $800 for it but of course I know that I cannot demand more than others whose property is just as valuable. If in any way the College could buy the lot for more, please let me know at once, as I have received an offer since I received your letter. It was is for $800.00 cash but I want to get $850.00 if possible. Enclosed find stamp. Truly yours,
>   Gertrude A. Mahorney, 263 Eliot St.” 
> 
> **April 30, 1915 Letter from President Howe:**
> - “I have your letter of April 27th and answering at once. The college does not buy real estate and could not, therefore, buy your lot. We are seeking to sell all real estate we hold for college purposes. If you can get $800.00 for your lot, let me advise you to accept it. If I owned the lot, I would certainly let it go at that price. I am very sure that this is a very substantial value for it.  If I can be of any assistance to you, I shall be glad to do so.  
>   Very truly yours. 
>   TCH/BH”
